# Where the atlas could go

Edition 003 is the completed current project: three worlds, reproducible experiments, two-future comparisons, and spatial difference maps. The ideas below are optional future directions, not unfinished release requirements or promises of background work.

## Edition 002: two possible worlds — complete

The split field now forks any live moment into two independent engines, varies one rule, synchronizes clocks and inputs, measures divergence, and saves both futures in a compact version 2 format. Single-world version 1 moments remain readable.

## Edition 003: see the divergence — complete

Chemical difference heatmaps, four-state cell occupancy maps, and wrapping traveler paths now show *where* futures diverge. Fixed labeled scales and display-only contrast avoid automatic normalization. Separate PNG exports preserve the legend and observation metadata.

Guided questions could help people form more specific experiments. A parameter history could distinguish a fixed-rule comparison from a sequence of live interventions.

## A better field notebook

Named observations, small thumbnails, IndexedDB for larger archives, and a time-stamped record of parameter changes could turn saved moments into a useful research journal. Migration must preserve existing browser notebooks.

## New chapters

- **A sandpile:** local toppling and avalanches at the edge of stability.
- **Excitable media:** waves that travel, collide, and spiral.
- **A gravitational dance:** orbital structure, with energy error made visible.

Each should be more than another animation: a clear question, a controllable model, repeatable experiments, and some way to recognize a surprising result.

## Deeper accessibility and performance

An interaction cursor that can be moved with arrow keys, optional sonification of aggregate statistics, and a worker-based engine for larger grids would make the observatory usable in more situations. Measure and test before adding architecture.
